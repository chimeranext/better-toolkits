## 1. What an insight is, and the four things it keeps getting confused with

An insight is a non-obvious truth about the world that, once stated, forces a different allocation of money, time or attention. One sentence. Present tense. No brand name in it.

Four impostors show up constantly. Learn to recognize them on sight.

**The observation.** True, visible, and known to everyone. "People spend more time on their phones than ever." Nothing follows from it that was not already being done.

**The restatement.** The Problem with different vocabulary. If the Problem is "developers cannot carry their reputation between platforms" and the Insight is "reputation is trapped inside platforms", you have written the same sentence twice and the piece has no turn. This is the most common failure in real drafts and the gate flags it with a lexical overlap check.

**The benefit.** A description of what your product does, wearing an insight costume. "Teams work better when they can see the whole pipeline" is a feature brochure with the serial numbers filed off.

**The aspiration.** A value statement. "Everyone deserves access to capital." Nobody disagrees, so nothing is at stake, so no allocation changes.

The diagnostic that separates all four from the real thing: **an insight has an enemy.** Somebody competent, well-funded and sincere is currently acting as if it were false. Name that person or that behavior. If you cannot, you have an impostor.

## 2. The seven generation moves

Generate at least five candidates. Use different moves for different candidates; running the same move five times produces five versions of one thought.

**Move 1. Consensus inversion.** Write the sentence the whole category believes and treats as settled. Then assume the opposite is operationally true and ask what would have to be the case for that to work. This is the Thiel move: what important truth do very few people agree with you on.

**Move 2. The unintended consequence.** Take the category's best practice, the thing everyone is proud of, and ask what it reliably produces that nobody counts. Safety warnings that produce tuning out. Personalization that produces sameness. Transparency that produces performance rather than honesty.

**Move 3. The paradox hunt.** Find where more of X produces less of X. More choice, less deciding. More reach, less attention. More metrics, less judgment. More funding, less urgency. Paradoxes are dense with insight because they are structurally invisible to anyone optimizing linearly.

**Move 4. The domain transplant.** What is this problem structurally identical to in a field that shares no vocabulary with it? Immune systems, credit ratings, monastic orders, air traffic control, sourdough starters, insurance underwriting. The transplant is not a metaphor for the pitch, it is a thinking tool: borrow the other field's solved constraint and see what it implies here.

**Move 5. The ugly truth.** What does the buyer actually optimize for that they will never say on a call? Career safety. Looking sophisticated to their boss. Not being blamed. Avoiding a conversation with legal. Most category communication addresses the stated motive and loses to whoever addresses the real one.

**Move 6. Temporal inversion.** Two directions. What is heresy today and will be obvious in five years? And, more useful because it is less crowded: what was true, quietly stopped being true, and everyone is still acting on it? The second produces insights with immediate commercial consequence, because the whole category is still paying for a constraint that lifted.

**Move 7. The denominator move.** The category argues about the numerator. Change what is being divided by. Cost per lead becomes cost per closed revenue. Hours worked becomes decisions made. Followers becomes people who would notice if you stopped. When the denominator moves, the ranking of every competitor moves with it, and that reordering is the insight.

## 3. The four tests

Every candidate that survives generation gets all four. One failure kills it.

**Inversion.** Negate the sentence. If the negation is absurd, you wrote an observation, because only observations have absurd negations. If the negation is a strategy that a competent, well-capitalized competitor could actually run, you wrote an insight. Both sides being live is the signature.

**Ownership.** Could three competitors sign the same sentence without flinching? Then it is category wallpaper. An insight that everyone can claim is not a secret, it is a shared assumption.

**Consequence.** Does the sentence force a reallocation? Name the specific thing that gets defunded, delayed or killed if it is true. An insight that changes no line in a budget is trivia, however elegant.

**Retroactive obviousness.** After hearing it, does the room feel it should have known? That feeling is the fingerprint. Novelty without recognition is noise. Recognition without novelty is a truism. The insight sits in the narrow band where both fire at once.

## 4. The scoring gate

Rate each candidate 1 to 10 on non-obviousness, where the question being scored is: how likely is it that a smart, well-briefed person in this category would have written this sentence unprompted?

- **1 to 4.** They would write it in the first minute. Kill.
- **5 to 6.** They would get there in an hour of thinking. Kill.
- **7 to 8.** A good strategist gets there in a week. Still kill. This band is where competent, forgettable work lives, and it is the band that most drafts settle into because it feels safe.
- **9.** Most would not get there, and everyone recognizes it as true once they hear it. Ship.
- **10.** Rare. It reorganizes how the category sees itself.

**Below 9 does not ship.** This threshold is the whole discipline. Softening it to 8 because time is short is the single most expensive shortcut available in this work, because everything downstream (the Big Idea, the Execution, the deck, the campaign, the raise) is built on that sentence.

If nothing clears 9 after two generation rounds, the failure is upstream. The Problem is at the wrong altitude, or the ground truth is too thin. Go back and raise the Problem rather than lowering the gate.

**Show your work.** When collaborating with a user, present the discarded candidates with their scores. Three dead 7s and one live 9 proves the search happened. A single candidate presented as final reads as the first thing that came to mind, and usually is.

## 5. Forbidden constructions

These openings signal that no search occurred. The gate flags them.

- "In today's fast-paced world..." and any variant of "in a world where"
- "Consumers are increasingly..." / "Los consumidores cada vez más..."
- "People don't want X, they want Y." Allowed only when Y is genuinely surprising. Nine times out of ten it produces a truism wearing a bow tie ("people don't want drills, they want holes" was an insight in 1960 and is a fortune cookie now).
- "It's not about X, it's about Y." Same problem, same exception.
- "At the end of the day..."
- "Everyone knows that..." An insight is the opposite of what everyone knows.
- Any sentence containing your brand name. The Insight belongs to the world, not to you. Your ownership of it shows up in B, not I.
- Any sentence with two independent clauses joined by "and". Two thoughts stapled together means you have not chosen. Choose.

## 6. Worked failures and repairs

**Failure: the restatement.**
Problem: Latin American developers build world-class software and are paid a fraction of the rate.
Insight (bad, 3/10): Latin American talent is undervalued by the global market.
Diagnosis: same sentence, second haircut. No turn. Nothing follows.
Insight (repaired, 9/10): The discount is not on the talent, it is on the verification. Buyers are not paying less for the work, they are paying less because they cannot check it, and nobody has ever tried to sell the checking.
Why it clears: it has an enemy (every marketplace competing on cheaper rates), it forces a reallocation (stop selling rate, build the proof layer), and it is retroactively obvious.

**Failure: the benefit in costume.**
Problem: Brand teams cannot tell whether their brand is working until a quarterly report arrives.
Insight (bad, 4/10): Brands need real-time visibility into their health.
Diagnosis: this is the product description. The enemy test fails; nobody is arguing for less visibility.
Insight (repaired, 9/10): Brand diagnosis fails not because the data is late but because it is unfalsifiable. A score nobody can lose is a score nobody acts on.
Why it clears: it attacks the category's own instrument, and the consequence is concrete, since it forces a deterministic scoring engine rather than a dashboard.

**Failure: the aspiration.**
Problem: Small event organizers cannot prove attendance to sponsors.
Insight (bad, 2/10): Trust is the foundation of every sponsorship relationship.
Diagnosis: nobody disagrees, therefore nothing is at stake.
Insight (repaired, 9/10): Sponsors do not buy attendance, they buy the story they can tell afterwards, and every fraudulent number in this industry exists because the story is due before the proof is possible.
Why it clears: it reframes the buyer's real motive (the ugly truth move), and it relocates the product from counting to timing.

**Failure: the 7 that felt like a 9.**
Insight (7/10): AI has made software cheap to build, so the bottleneck moved to knowing what to build.
Diagnosis: true, consequential, and currently being said on roughly four hundred podcasts. Fails ownership, since every AI-native shop can sign it.
The repair is not to polish this sentence. It is to run move 6 or move 7 on the same territory and find what the four hundred podcasts are still assuming.

## 7. Insight for non-commercial subjects

The node does not change when the subject is a foundation, a policy, an internal reorganization or a personal essay. What changes is where the consensus lives.

For institutional and government work, the consensus is usually procedural rather than commercial: everyone agrees on the goal and the disagreement is buried in the mechanism. The insight is almost always in the mechanism.

For internal communication, the consensus is what the team has stopped questioning because it was true when the company was a third of its current size. Temporal inversion (move 6, second direction) is the highest-yield move inside an organization, and it is also the most uncomfortable one, which is why it is available.

For an essay, the enemy test still applies and is what separates an essay from a blog post. If no reasonable reader could disagree, you have written a summary.
