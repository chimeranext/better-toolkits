## 1. The classic: a public safety campaign

The canonical teaching example, because every node is visible and the causal chain is clean. Melbourne's rail safety campaign, 2012.

**P.** People were dying in preventable accidents around trains, and the audience most at risk was the least reachable. The category consensus: safety communication works by making people afraid. Every rail authority in the world was buying the same grim imagery, and the target audience had learned to look away from it. The cost was measured in lives and had been flat for years, which is the strongest possible setup, since a flat line invites the question of why nobody had solved it.

**I.** Humor reaches people that fear cannot, and the most preventable deaths are the stupidest ones.
Score: 9. Inversion holds, since "fear reaches people humor cannot" is exactly what every competent safety authority was funding. Ownership holds, since no competitor could have signed it without abandoning their entire creative approach. Consequence is severe, in that it defunds the whole grim-imagery apparatus. Retroactive obviousness fires hard.

**B.** Dumb Ways to Die.
Poster test: survives, and it survives without a logo. Logo swap: another rail authority using it would be visibly borrowing. Verb test: it implies an action, in that it invites people to enumerate stupid ways to die, which is precisely the participation mechanic the campaign ran on.

**E.** An animated song with cheerful characters dying absurdly, released as a music video rather than as an ad. Distribution through music platforms and a game, so the work lived where the audience already was rather than where safety messaging usually sits. A pledge mechanic converting attention into commitment. Merchandise and toys, which is the ritual surface, since a campaign that becomes an object outlives its media buy. Every element traces to the Insight: humor is the medium, absurdity is the argument, and entertainment channels are the only place the humor could function.

**R.** Business tier: a documented reduction in accidents and near-misses on the network. Behavior tier: millions of pledges taken. Category tier: it changed what safety communication is allowed to look like, worldwide, permanently, which is the rarest and most valuable result a piece of work can produce.

Note what makes this teachable: the Insight is a sentence about human nature, not about trains. That is why the Big Idea could go anywhere the Insight was true.

## 2. The venture pitch with no traction

Same discipline, but Results have not happened. This is the shape most founders actually need.

**P.** Software companies in emerging markets sell hours. Buyers in developed markets buy hours because they have no other way to price the work. The consensus, held by every marketplace, agency and outsourcing firm in the category: the way to win is a lower rate. The cost is structural, in that the entire region is locked into a discount that no amount of skill escapes, and it has held for two decades, which is the setup.

**I.** The discount is not on the talent, it is on the verification. Buyers are not paying less for the work, they are paying less because they cannot check it, and nobody has ever tried to sell the checking.
Score: 9. The negation ("the discount is on the talent") is exactly what the category funds, which is the inversion passing. No competitor competing on rate can sign it. It forces a total reallocation, from sales capacity into proof infrastructure. And it is retroactively obvious, since anyone who has hired remotely has felt the verification gap without ever naming it as the price.

**B.** Proof, not price.
Traces to the Insight directly. Survives a poster. A rate-based competitor using the line would be arguing against their own business model, which is the logo swap passing. It implies a move: stop bidding, start issuing evidence.

**E.** Shipped: the verified record of what each builder has done, portable across engagements. The delivery model that produces the evidence as a byproduct of the work rather than as reporting overhead. Pricing per outcome rather than per hour, which is the price surface making the argument. A partner stack whose names are themselves verification. Committed and marked as such: the buyer-facing view of the record, and the third-party audit of the scoring method. Cut from this section: a marketplace directory, which traces to a distribution instinct rather than to the Insight, and its removal is worth naming out loud.

**R.** The falsifiable bet. By [date], [N] engagements closed without a rate negotiation, at a gross margin of [X] against a category norm of [Y]. If the rate conversation happens anyway on more than half of them, the Insight is wrong and the pricing model goes back in the box.

The bet is the node. It reads as more credible than a hockey stick because it names the condition under which the founder would admit the thesis failed, and almost nobody does that.

## 3. The transposed essay

Same spine, no labels anywhere, and the framework should be undetectable to a reader.

The subject: an essay arguing that fear is a usable engine and an unusable fuel.

**Rendered opening (P, invisible).** Open on a scene where fear is producing excellent work, because the reader has to feel the tension between the output and the cost before any argument arrives. No thesis in the first paragraph. The consensus being challenged (that fear is simply bad for performance, and separately, that whatever works should be kept) is left implicit and named in the second paragraph.

**The turn (I).** End of paragraph two or three, one sentence. Fear is an excellent engine and a terrible fuel: it will start anything and it will burn the thing it started. The entire essay's value sits in this line, so it gets the most rewriting and the least surrounding text.

**The coined frame (B).** In an essay, the Big Idea is a term. Engine versus fuel is the term, and the essay's job for the next thousand words is to make it load-bearing enough that the reader uses it later without attribution.

**The argument (E).** The evidence, the counterexamples, the mechanism. Every section traces to the distinction, and anything that is merely interesting about fear gets cut. In essays, untraced material is the most common failure, because interesting things are easy to write and hard to delete.

**The implication (R).** Not a metric. What changes if the frame is true: which decisions get made differently, and what the reader should stop doing on Monday. The essay's Results node is always a consequence, never a number.

At no point does the reader encounter a header called Insight. They encounter a turn, and they feel it.

## 4. What the three have in common

The Insight in all three is a sentence about how people or markets work, not about the product, the campaign or the author. That is the portable rule.

In all three, the Big Idea is shorter than five words and contains a move. In all three, at least one thing was visibly cut from Execution, because a section with nothing removed suggests nothing was chosen. And in all three, the Results node answers the question the Problem raised, which is the backwards check that closes the loop.
