# Failure Modes and the Audit Rubric

Read in audit mode, and read before presenting any board. Most PIBER drafts fail in one of twelve identifiable ways, and eleven of them are diagnosable from the text alone.

2. The 100-point rubric

## 1. The twelve failure modes

**F1. Company altitude.** The Problem is a task on someone's roadmap rather than a condition in the world. Symptom: the first paragraph contains the company name. Repair: write the Problem again for a reader who has never heard of the company and has no reason to care.

**F2. The double P.** The Insight restates the Problem in new vocabulary. There is no turn, so the piece flattens and the audience waits for something that never arrives. This is the single most common failure in real drafts. Repair: run the enemy test on the Insight. If nobody competent is acting as if it were false, it is not an insight.

**F3. The benefit in costume.** The Insight describes what the product does. Symptom: the sentence would appear on the homepage under a checkmark. Repair: strip every product noun out of the sentence and see whether anything survives.

**F4. The 7 that shipped.** The Insight is true, reasonable, and available to every competitor. It passes casual reading and fails ownership. Repair: do not polish it. Regenerate with a different move from the forge.

**F5. Tagline without idea.** B is a line nobody can act on. Symptom: asked what the company should therefore do differently on Monday, the room goes quiet. Repair: state the move, then compress it into a line, in that order.

**F6. Feature list as Big Idea.** B is three capabilities joined by commas. Repair: the Big Idea is one move, and features are E.

**F7. Execution as roadmap.** E is what will happen rather than what happened. Legitimate for pre-launch work, fatal when it hides a lack of shipped proof. Repair: separate shipped from committed explicitly, and move the burden to a falsifiable bet in R.

**F8. Untraced execution.** E contains elements that do not descend from the Insight. Symptom: the section would be identical if the Insight were replaced with a different one. Repair: draw the arrows, cut what does not trace, and show the cut list.

**F9. Activity metrics.** R leads with impressions, followers, sessions or posts. Repair: find the behavior that changed, or the money, or admit the result is not yet in and convert to a bet.

**F10. Naked numbers.** Results without denominators or a before. "40,000 users" answers nothing until it is against a market, a period or a starting point. Repair: every number gets a denominator and a baseline.

**F11. Scaffolding left on the building.** The rendered artifact has slides or headers named Problem, Insight, Big Idea. Repair: delete the labels, write the turn as a sentence, give each section a real title.

**F12. Correct and dead.** All five nodes present, all tests passed, and nobody would repeat a word of it. The framework is a guarantee of soundness, not of life. Repair: the turn, the Big Idea line and the close are the three places where craft is non-optional. Rewrite those three and leave the rest.

## 2. The 100-point rubric

Weighted from the Cannes Creative Strategy allocation, adjusted so Execution carries its own proof burden.

**Problem, 25 points**
- Category altitude rather than company altitude (10)
- The consensus being challenged is stated explicitly (5)
- The cost of the status quo is quantified or vividly held (5)
- A stranger feels the tension in the first three sentences (5)

**Insight, 30 points**
- One sentence, no brand name (5)
- Passes inversion: the negation is a live strategy (8)
- Passes ownership: competitors could not sign it (7)
- Passes consequence: it forces a reallocation (5)
- Passes retroactive obviousness (5)

**Big Idea, 20 points**
- Traces directly to the Insight (6)
- Survives the poster test (5)
- Fails the logo swap, which is the pass condition here (5)
- Contains or implies a move, not just a state (4)

**Execution, 15 points**
- Proof rather than plan, or the split is declared (5)
- Every element traces to the Insight (6)
- At least three of the six surfaces are covered (4)

**Results, 10 points**
- Denominators and baselines present (4)
- At least two of the three tiers (business, behavior, category) (3)
- No activity metric as headline (3)

**Reading the score.** Below 60, the argument is broken and needs a rebuild from the Problem, not an edit. 60 to 74, the spine holds and the Insight is usually the leak. 75 to 89, shippable, and the remaining points are craft. 90 plus, ship it and stop touching it.

**One override.** An Insight scoring under 20 of its 30 caps the total at 74 regardless of everything else. A beautiful deck on a weak insight is a beautiful deck on a weak insight, and the rubric should not launder that.

## 3. How to deliver an audit

Deliver a memo, not a rival document. The user asked what is broken, not for a replacement they did not commission.

Structure, in this order: the score with the band, the one thing that is working, the leak (the single highest-value repair, named precisely), the secondary repairs as a short list, and what a rebuild would cost if the leak is upstream in the Problem.

Two disciplines. Quote the draft's own lines when naming a failure, since a diagnosis without evidence reads as taste. And name the one repair that matters most rather than distributing attention evenly across twelve; the value of an audit is prioritization, not inventory.

If the user then wants the rebuild, that is a new mode and it starts at the Problem.

## 4. The two-minute triage

For a fast read on any pitch, deck or essay, in order. Most drafts fail before question four.

1. Underline the Insight. Is there exactly one sentence you could underline? If not, F2 or F3.
2. Negate it. Is the negation absurd? If yes, F2.
3. Could a competitor sign it? If yes, F4.
4. Put the Big Idea on a poster. Does it survive alone? If not, F5 or F6.
5. Swap the logo. Does it still work? If yes, the idea belongs to the category.
6. Pick any three Execution elements. Do they trace to the Insight? If not, F8.
7. Read the Results out loud. Is the first number a denominator away from meaning nothing? If yes, F10.
8. Scan the headers. Do any say "Insight"? If yes, F11.
