# Skill `strategy-to-board` — protocol SSOT

## Purpose

Transfer the technology strategy from the fractional CTO to the client's CEO
and Board. The goal is that the Board owns the strategy as a decision tool —
roadmap, portfolio, bets, and risk — with every initiative traced to a
business outcome, after the CTO steps back.

Grounded in the CTO-role references (MIT CTO Program roadmapping/portfolio,
Devox Visionary, amazingCTO) and the organic-executive handover flow: the
strategy is presented to the ultimate owners and accepted by them.

## What is handed over

1. **Strategy statement.** The technology strategy in one page: the business
   outcomes it serves, the bets it makes, and the logic connecting them.
2. **Roadmap.** Horizons (H1/H2/H3 — see `strategy/roadmap`) with initiatives,
   owners, dependencies, and measures. State explicitly what is committed vs.
   speculative.
3. **Portfolio and investment asks.** Where budget and talent are allocated
   (see `strategy/portfolio`), the asks the Board must endorse, and the
   tradeoffs made.
4. **R&D alignment.** How innovation pipeline choices align to business goals
   (see `strategy/rnd-alignment`).
5. **Risks and uncertainties.** The key assumptions, the risks that could
   invalidate the strategy, and the triggers that should cause a revisit.
6. **Open decisions.** Items awaiting a Board decision, each with options,
   recommendation, and urgency.

## To whom

The CEO and Board (the organic owners of strategy). The CEO is the point of
delivery; the Board is the decision-making audience.

## Delivery mechanism

1. **Assemble the handover packet** in the engagement workspace (for example,
   `./fractional-cto/handover/strategy-to-board/<date>/`): strategy statement,
   roadmap, portfolio, risks, open decisions.
2. **Present to the Board** (or a structured CEO briefing) — not an email.
   Walk through the bets, the tradeoffs, the risk, and the decisions requested.
3. **Obtain explicit acceptance.** The Board acknowledges the strategy and
   decides on the open items. Unresolved decisions remain visible as open items
   with owners — they are not silently closed.
4. **Hand the cadence to the org.** Identify who owns refreshing the roadmap on
   the quarterly cadence going forward.
5. **Re-confirm after a transition period** that the strategy continues to be
   governed without the CTO.

## Contract

- Every initiative traces to a business outcome; anything that cannot is
   explicitly parked or removed.
- Bets and risks are stated plainly — the Board cannot own what it has not
   been told.
- Open decisions are delivered with options and a recommendation, not dumped as
   open questions.
- The roadmap is a living, owned artifact with a cadence, not a one-time
   document.
