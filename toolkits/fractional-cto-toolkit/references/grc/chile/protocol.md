## Current state

Chile is in a **legal transition** on data protection:

- The historic law is **Ley 19.628** (1999) on protection of private life and
  personal data — limited in scope and enforcement.
- In December 2024, Chile enacted **Ley 21.719** (the data protection reform),
  which creates a dedicated regulator — the **Agencia de Protección de Datos
  Personales** — and modernizes principles, rights, and sanctions. The law has
  an extended transition period and is expected to apply fully in 2026–2027
  depending on the phasing of obligations.

## Preliminary applicable frameworks

- Ley 19.628 (in force today): consent, use restrictions, and the action to
  protect personal data.
- Ley 21.719 (reform, phasing in): controller/processor roles, GDPR-like
  principles, data subject rights (access, rectification, deletion,
  portability, opposition), mandatory security measures, and the Agency's
  powers.
- Sectoral: financial (CMF — Comisión para el Mercado Financiero), health,
  telecom (SUBTEL).

## What a fractional CTO should do

1. Keep Ley 19.628 compliance in place while tracking the Ley 21.719
   transition calendar.
2. Proactively adopt the reform's principles (data minimization, purpose
   limitation, privacy by design) since they will become mandatory.
3. Prepare for the new Agency: DPO-equivalent roles, records, and sanction
   exposure.
4. Apply sectoral rules where the client is regulated.
