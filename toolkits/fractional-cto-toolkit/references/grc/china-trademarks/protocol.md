# Skill `china-trademarks` — protocol SSOT

## Purpose

Build a **defensive China trademark strategy** for importers who source goods
from Chinese factories (white-label/OEM). China operates a **first-to-file**
regime: whoever files first — the importer *or the factory* — owns the mark.
The strategy is to register the brand in China **before** engaging the factory
or sharing brand/design IP, so the OEM cannot register the mark first and
block the import or appropriate the brand.

Grounded in the CTO-role references: Corsearch (first-to-file vs.
first-to-use), Woodburn (China trademark rules update), Trademark Partners
(first-to-file), CBL Translations (China trademarks FAQ), and China Briefing
(register before market entry). Sushegaad GRC as benchmark only.

## When to use

- A client plans a white-label agreement with a Chinese manufacturer.
- A client already imports from China but has not registered its mark there.
- A client discovered that a factory or third party has filed its mark in
  China (conflict response).

## Method

1. **Clear the mark first**: run a China clearance search (CNIPA and
   marketplace use) before filing; note that China's prior-use defenses are
   limited, so filing is the priority.
2. **Decide the filing route**:
   - **Madrid Protocol**: designate China through the international system —
     cheaper for multi-country coverage, but China is a designated office
     with its own examination.
   - **Direct National Filing**: file with the CNIPA through a licensed local
     agent — faster, more flexible on class selection, and generally the
     recommended route when China is the strategic market.
   Recommend based on market entry speed, class strategy, and budget; a
   licensed local agent is required for direct filing.
3. **Select classes (CNIPA/Nice sub-classes)**: cover not only the goods
   themselves but related classes the factory or marketplace could occupy —
   e.g., the goods class, packaging, retail/wholesale services (Class 35),
   and any adjacent services. Map the product line to the specific Nice
   classes and sub-classes (e.g., Class 9 for electronics, Class 25 for
   apparel, Class 29/30 for food).
4. **File before engagement**: complete filing before sharing brand/design IP
   or placing purchase orders. Sequence: clearance → filing → then contract.
5. **Include IP terms in the OEM agreement**: non-use and no-filing clauses,
   assignment/back-assignment duties, and confidentiality around the designs.
6. **Monitor**: watch CNIPA filings and marketplace listings for conflicts;
   renew marks on the 10-year cycle.
7. **Enforce if preempted**: if the factory (or a squatter) filed first,
   pursue invalidation of the bad-faith registration (under the Trademark Law
   rules on bad-faith filing) and opposition routes with local counsel.

## Artifact

A China trademark pack: clearance results, filing-route recommendation
(Madrid vs. direct), class map, filing timeline, OEM agreement clauses, a
watch schedule, and an enforcement playbook.

## Contract

- The mark is filed **before** brand/design IP is shared with the factory.
- Class strategy is explicit and matches the actual product line and go-to-
  market channels.
- Registry decisions are executed through a licensed CNIPA agent; this skill
  does not substitute for trademark counsel.
- Vendor-neutral and client-neutral; no brand or client names.
- Handover: on engagement end, transfer to the Chief Compliance Officer (CCO)
  — filing status, class map, watch items.
