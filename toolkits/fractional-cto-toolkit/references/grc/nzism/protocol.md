# Skill `nzism` — protocol SSOT

## Purpose

Assess and build a cybersecurity posture against the New Zealand Information
Security Manual (NZISM), published by the Government Communications Security
Bureau (GCSB). The NZISM is the control framework for New Zealand government
agencies and a strong baseline for NZ industry.

Grounded in the CTO-role references (Splunk, Devox; Sushegaad GRC as benchmark
only).

## When to use

- A client supplies systems or services to NZ government agencies.
- A client is an NZ agency modernizing its security posture.
- A client wants an NZ-appropriate baseline aligned to government practice.

## Method

1. **Scope the system** and its security requirements per the NZISM model
   (protective security requirements — people, information, ICT, physical).
2. **Map NZISM controls**: use the NZISM's control framework (numbered
   controls across management, physical, and ICT-information security
   domains) with the maturity levels (Levels 1–5) that define how well each
   control is implemented.
3. **Assess current state** per control: determine the maturity level in
   place and the target level required by the system's classification and
   business risk.
4. **Gap analysis**: identify controls below target; produce a prioritized
   remediation plan.
5. **Implement and evidence**: close gaps and collect evidence per control.
6. **Maintain**: reassess on change or on the NZISM update cycle.

## Artifact

An NZISM pack: system scope, control mapping with maturity levels, gap
assessment, and a remediation plan.

## Contract

- Maturity claims follow the NZISM definitions; targets are risk-based.
- Every control has an owner and evidence.
- Vendor-neutral and client-neutral.
- Handover: on engagement end, transfer to the Chief Compliance Officer (CCO)
  — mappings, maturities, open items.
- Verify against the current NZISM edition and GCSB guidance.
