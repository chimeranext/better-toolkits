# Skill `cyber-essentials` — protocol SSOT

## Purpose

Prepare an organization for the UK **Cyber Essentials** certification — the
government-backed baseline security scheme (IASME, backed by the NCSC). It is
often required for UK government contracts and increasingly by insurers. The
fractional CTO drives the technical readiness and the evidence.

Grounded in the CTO-role references (Splunk, Devox; Sushegaad GRC as benchmark
only).

## When to use

- A UK client bids for government or public-sector work requiring Cyber
  Essentials.
- A client needs the scheme for cyber insurance or customer assurance.
- A client wants a low-friction baseline before a fuller ISMS.

## Method

1. **Scope the boundary**: define the systems in scope (all internet-facing
   and user devices relevant to the assessment boundary).
2. **Verify the five technical controls**:
   - **Firewalls** — boundary and device firewalls enabled, configured, and
     managed.
   - **Secure configuration** — default passwords and unnecessary services
     removed; configuration hardened.
   - **User access control** — user accounts, least privilege, and the
     removal of unused accounts.
   - **Malware protection** — anti-malware on all applicable devices.
   - **Patch management** — security patches applied; supported software
     only.
3. **Complete the self-assessment questionnaire**: answer honestly — the
   scheme's assurance is contingent on accurate self-certification.
4. **Cyber Essentials Plus**: if the client needs Plus, prepare for the
   external vulnerability scan and the onsite assessment by a certification
   body.
5. **Fill gaps and evidence**: implement fixes and capture evidence for each
   control (configs, inventories, patch status).
6. **Certify and maintain**: submit through a certification body; renew
   annually and keep the environment compliant between renewals.

## Artifact

A Cyber Essentials pack: boundary scope, control-by-control status, questionnaire
sign-off, gap list, and the evidence folder for Plus.

## Contract

- The self-assessment reflects the real environment; no misleading
   declarations.
- Every device in scope is accounted for in the five controls.
- Vendor-neutral and client-neutral.
- Handover: on engagement end, transfer to the Chief Compliance Officer (CCO)
  — controls, evidence, certification status.
- Verify against the current Cyber Essentials requirements (including the
   latest control refinements from the NCSC/IASME).
