# Skill `china-pipl` — protocol SSOT

## Purpose

Assess and support compliance with China's tripartite data regulatory regime:
the **Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL)**, the **Data Security Law
(DSL)**, and the **Cybersecurity Law (CSL)**, together with the implementing
measures (e.g., network data security management measures and cross-border
transfer rules). It matters to any client processing personal data in or from
China — including foreign companies with Chinese users, suppliers, or
subsidiaries.

Grounded in the CTO-role references, including DataGuidance (interplay of
PIPL/DSL/CSL) and Latham & Watkins (Network Data Security Management
Regulations); Sushegaad GRC as benchmark only.

## When to use

- A client processes personal data of individuals in China.
- A client has a Chinese entity, users, or a supply chain crossing China.
- A client must move personal data out of China (cross-border transfer).

## Method

1. **Inventory and map**: identify personal information (PI), important data,
   and sensitive PI; document purposes, legal bases, and the actors
   (handler, entrusting party, recipient).
2. **Apply the three laws to the data type**:
   - **PIPL** governs PI processing: consent and the other legal bases,
     separate consent for sensitive PI and for transfers, individual rights
     (access, correction, deletion, portability, explanation), and the
     requirement for a  in charge (or an overseas entity's representative in
     China) where volume thresholds are met.
   - **DSL** governs data security classification and important-data
     protection, including local storage of important data and security
     assessments for its export.
   - **CSL** governs network/cyber security obligations for network operators
     and critical information infrastructure (CII), including local storage
     of PI collected in China.
3. **Cross-border transfer routes** (for PI): one of the three routes —
   (a) **security assessment** for CII operators and handlers meeting volume
   thresholds, (b) **standard contract filing** (SCC) with the CAC, or
   (c) **certification** by an accredited body. Verify the current rules on
   which route applies, including the amended standard-contract measures.
4. **Impact assessments**: perform the required self-assessments (PI
   protection impact assessment) before cross-border transfers and before
   handling sensitive data.
5. **Security and breach**: implement security measures (CSL/DSL basis) and
   follow the breach-notification duties under the PIPL.
6. **Records and local presence**: retain records, honor data subject
   requests, and keep the local representative/DPO appointment current.

## Artifact

A China data compliance pack: data inventory (PI/sensitive/important), legal
basis register, cross-border transfer route memo (which route applies and
why), impact-assessment register, and records.

## Contract

- The cross-border transfer analysis is written as a recommendation pending
  CAC confirmation and local counsel review; the landscape changes as
  implementing rules evolve.
- Important-data classification is verified with local counsel — it is
  sector-specific.
- Vendor-neutral and client-neutral.
- Handover: on engagement end, transfer to the Chief Compliance Officer (CCO)
  — inventory, transfer routes, open items.
- Track the latest CAC and MIIT measures; treat the 2025 amendments to the
  standard-contract measures as the current reference until superseded.
