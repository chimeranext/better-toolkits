## Current state

Costa Rica has **no comprehensive general data protection law** in force.
A data protection bill has circulated in the Legislative Assembly for years,
but Costa Rica has not enacted a general law like GDPR or LGPD.

What exists today:

- **Constitutional right of habeas data** (Constitución Política, Art. 27):
  a constitutional remedy to access, correct, and protect personal information.
- **Sectoral rules**:
  - Financial: banking secrecy and SUGEF (Superintendencia General de
    Entidades Financieras) regulations.
  - Health: personal health data handling rules.
  - Telecommunications: SUTEL (Superintendencia de Telecomunicaciones)
    consumer/information rules.
  - Public sector: general administrative transparency rules (Ley de
    Transparencia y Acceso a la Información Pública).

## Preliminary applicable frameworks

- For the public sector, the transparency law governs access to public
  information and imposes records obligations.
- For private-sector processing, in the absence of a general law, rely on
  international best practice (ISO 27701, GDPR as benchmark), constitutional
  habeas data, and sectoral regulators.

## What a fractional CTO should do

1. Treat the market expectation as de facto GDPR-like (large clients and
   multinational customers expect it).
2. Implement a baseline privacy program (records, legal bases, security,
   breach handling) regardless of the legal gap.
3. Track ongoing legislative movement toward a general data protection law.
4. Apply sectoral rules when the client is regulated (financial, health,
   telecom).
