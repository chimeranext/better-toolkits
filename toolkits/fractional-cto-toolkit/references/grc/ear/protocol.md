# Skill `ear` — protocol SSOT

## Purpose

Assess and support US export compliance under the Export Administration
Regulations (EAR), which govern dual-use items, software, and technology.
The fractional CTO builds the classification, screening, and process layer;
legal export decisions are confirmed with trade counsel.

Grounded in the CTO-role references (Splunk, Devox; Sushegaad GRC as benchmark
only).

## When to use

- A client exports goods, software, or technology from the US.
- A client ships to partners, shares technical data, or employs foreign
  nationals (deemed-export exposure).
- A client is asked to certify export compliance by a customer or a bank.

## Method

1. **Classify the items**: determine the Export Control Classification Number
   (ECCN) from the Commerce Control List (CCL), or confirm EAR99 if no ECCN
   applies. This is the foundation of everything else.
2. **Check destination, end-user, and end-use controls**: screen against
   denied/restricted party lists (BIS List of Denied Persons, Entity List,
   etc.), embargoed destinations (e.g., Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Syria,
   Crimea), and end-use restrictions (nuclear, missile, chemical/biological
   weapons).
3. **Evaluate licensing**: determine whether a license is required (e.g.,
   License Exception vs. a BIS license) for the classified item and the
   transaction.
4. **Address deemed exports**: US-origin technology released to foreign
   nationals inside the US may require a deemed-export license; cover it.
5. **Build screening workflow**: operationalize list screening at order entry
   (customer, bank, freight forwarder) with a documented process.
6. **Write the export compliance manual**: roles, procedures, recordkeeping
   (5-year retention), training, and internal reviews.
7. **Verify and maintain**: run periodic audits; update classification on
   regulatory changes.

## Artifact

An EAR compliance pack: ECCN/EAR99 classification record, destination/party
screening map, licensing determination, and the export compliance manual.

## Contract

- Classification is documented per item and revisited when the product or
  CCL changes.
- Screening is systematic, not ad hoc; results are logged.
- Vendor-neutral and client-neutral.
- Handover: on engagement end, transfer to the Chief Compliance Officer (CCO)
  — classifications, screening workflow, open items.
- Confirm licensing determinations with BIS or trade counsel before shipment.
