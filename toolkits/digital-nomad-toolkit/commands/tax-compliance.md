# /digital-nomad:tax-compliance

Guide to tax compliance for US Single-Member LLCs owned by non-residents.

## Audience

Freelancers and digital nomads with a US Single-Member LLC who need to stay tax-compliant.

## Non-resident tax implications

| Scenario | US tax |
|----------|--------|
| Non-resident, no US operations | Income may be non-taxable in the US |
| Non-resident, US operations | Subject to US taxes on US-sourced income |
| Tax treaty country | May reduce withholding taxes |

**Key principle**: If you're not a US tax resident and don't have a US place of business, your income may be non-taxable in the US. However, you may still need to file reports.

## Annual reporting obligations

| Report | Frequency | Where | Cost |
|--------|-----------|-------|------|
| **Annual report** | Annual | State Secretary of State | $0-$300 |
| **Franchise tax** | Annual | State tax authority | $0-$300 |
| **Federal tax return** | Annual | IRS | Free (if no tax due) |
| **State tax return** | Annual | State tax authority | Varies |
| **BOI Report** | One-time (update on changes) | FinCEN | Free |

## Tax treaties

Many countries have tax treaties with the US to avoid double taxation:

| Country | Treaty? | Effect |
|---------|---------|--------|
| Costa Rica | No | May need to pay taxes in both countries |
| Mexico | Yes | Reduces withholding taxes |
| Colombia | Yes | Reduces withholding taxes |
| Spain | Yes | Reduces withholding taxes |

## Home country tax obligations

| Country | Requirement |
|---------|-------------|
| Costa Rica | Declare foreign income, pay taxes on worldwide income |
| Mexico | Declare foreign income, pay taxes on worldwide income |
| Colombia | Declare foreign income, pay taxes on worldwide income |

**Important**: Even if your US LLC has no US tax obligation, you may still need to declare the income in your home country.
