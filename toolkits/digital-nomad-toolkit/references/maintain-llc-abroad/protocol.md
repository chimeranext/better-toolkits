# Protocol: maintain-llc-abroad (digital-nomad)

Keeping a Wyoming Single-Member LLC alive from a student/founder address
abroad.

## Annual checklist

| Item | Cadence | Notes |
|------|---------|-------|
| Registered agent | Annual | Wyoming renewal keeps the statutory address alive |
| Wyoming annual report | Annual | Secretary of State; minimum license tax |
| IRS filings | Annual | Per classification — zero-activity years still file |
| Banking address | On change | Keep the **US** virtual address; never a foreign residential one |
| Stripe profile | On change | US business address + payout account stay consistent |
| EIN address | On change | Form 8822-B if the address of record changes |
| Mail forwarding | Ongoing | Scan-and-forward for IRS/state mail; check monthly |
| Bookkeeping | Monthly | Clean zero-year returns beat reconstruction |

## Golden rule

**Maintenance is not self-employment.** Registrations, filings, and
zero-activity months are entity upkeep. Answering a client call, shipping,
or negotiating from the study/founder country crosses into that country's
activity rules.

## Address-change triggers

- US virtual-address provider change → agent, SOS, EIN (8822-B), bank,
  Stripe, client W-9s.
- Foreign housing moves → nothing in the US records.
- Bank KYC asks for residence: give the foreign address honestly in the KYC
  field while the business address stays US — never fabricate.

## Dormancy

- **Zero-activity year**: file zero returns, keep the agent paid — cheaper
  than dissolve-and-reform (new EIN, new banking/Stripe history ends
  permanently).
- Never let the SOS mark the LLC inactive — reinstatement fees + lost good
  standing ripple into banking.

## Platform hygiene

- Mercury/Relay: US address of record; foreign login geography triggers
  reviews — keep documents ready. Wise: personal vs business rules by
  country of residence. Stripe: consistency. US cards + persistent foreign
  IP = review risk.

## Disclaimer

This document is an educational guide. NOT legal, tax, or financial advice.
Confirm current requirements with your registered agent, bank, and tax
preparer.
