# Transposition

How PIBER renders into artifacts that are not case boards. This is the mode that makes the framework universal, and the mode where it most often shows through the wallpaper.

## Contents

1. The three rules of transposition
2. The map: 14 artifacts
3. The artifacts with real gotchas

## 1. The three rules of transposition

**Rule 1. The beats survive, the labels die.** Only award boards, internal strategy documents and teaching materials carry visible node names. Everywhere else the audience should feel the structure and never see it. A slide titled "Insight" tells a room that the next sentence was manufactured, which is exactly the wrong frame for the sentence you worked hardest on.

**Rule 2. The order can compress but not scramble.** In a cold email, P and I may share one sentence. In a landing page, R may appear as logos above the fold before the reader reaches P. That is placement, not reordering: the argument still runs P to R, and the early logos are a credential, not the Results node. What you may never do is deliver B before I has landed, because an idea without its insight is a slogan.

**Rule 3. Every artifact has a native opening and PIBER does not supply it.** A keynote opens with a hook. An essay opens with a scene or a claim. A cold email opens with the reason this specific person is reading. PIBER guarantees the spine is sound. The first fifteen words are still your job.

## 2. The map: 14 artifacts

| Artifact | P becomes | I becomes | B becomes | E becomes | R becomes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Investor deck (seed) | Market failure, why now | The thesis / the secret | The company in one line | Product, GTM, moat | Traction, or the falsifiable bet |
| Sales proposal / RFP | Their problem, in their words | What their category gets wrong | The engagement's organizing idea | Scope, method, team | Case evidence and the guarantee |
| Keynote / talk | The tension the room lives in | The turn | The one idea they repeat at lunch | Three stories or demos | What changes if they act |
| Essay / op-ed | The stakes | The thesis | The coined term or frame | Argument and evidence | The implication |
| Sponsorship deck | The sponsor's reach problem | What their category buys wrong | The property's organizing idea | Assets, tiers, activation | Audience proof, past ROI |
| Cold email | One line of their reality | One line they have not heard | Why you exist, in five words | One credential | One number, one ask |
| Speaker invitation | The moment the field is in | Why this stage is the argument | The name of the thing | Format, lineup, logistics | The lineage they join |
| Grant / government | The public failure | The mechanism nobody fixed | The program name | Implementation and partners | Measurable public outcome |
| Product launch | What was broken for users | Why it stayed broken | The release's organizing idea | What shipped | What is now possible |
| Internal memo / all-hands | The honest situation | What we have been assuming wrongly | The name of the shift | The plan and who owns it | What we will measure |
| Award submission | The business challenge | Strategic breakthrough | The creative idea | The work | Effectiveness data |
| Landing page | Headline tension | Subhead turn | The hero line | Feature blocks, proof strip | Metrics, testimonials |
| Social thread | Hook (the tension) | Post 2 or 3, the turn | The coined frame | Examples | The payoff and the CTA |
| One-line bio | Implied | Implied | The whole line | Implied | The credential clause |

## 3. The artifacts with real gotchas

**Investor deck.** The Insight is the thesis and it belongs on slide 2 or 3, never slide 12. Investors do not fund problems, they fund a differentiated view of a problem. The most common failure is fifteen slides of Execution with the Insight buried in a footnote, which reads as a competent team with no reason to exist. Results become the falsifiable bet when there is no traction: the number, the date, and what would prove you wrong. Do not soften it into a projection.

**Sales proposal.** P must be written in the client's vocabulary, ideally quoting their own language back from the discovery call. The Insight is where you earn the right to charge above cost: it is the sentence that says their category is doing this wrong, delivered without insulting the person who chose the current approach. Aim the criticism at the category's practice, never at the buyer's judgment.

**Keynote.** The clock changes the shape. In a 20-minute talk, P is 3 minutes, I is 90 seconds, B is 2 minutes, E is 10 minutes of stories, R is 3 minutes. The Insight is the only part the audience will quote, so it needs to be sayable out loud, which means no subordinate clauses and no more than about twenty words. Write it to be repeated at a coffee break, badly, by someone who half remembers it.

**Essay.** The Big Idea is usually a coined term. That is the essay's version of an ownable idea, and it is why essays that name something outlive essays that argue something. The Execution node becomes your evidence and argument, and the discipline is the same: every section traces to the Insight or it gets cut.

**Cold email and speaker invitation.** Compression to the point of near-invisibility. Total budget: 90 words. P and I fuse into one sentence, B is why you exist, E is one credential, R is one number and one ask. The rule that matters most here is status: write from equal standing, as a peer stating what is happening, never as a supplicant asking for a favor. A supplicant frame breaks the Insight node, because a person asking for a favor cannot credibly tell you what your category is getting wrong.

**Grant and government.** The Insight has to be procedural, since the goal is rarely contested and the mechanism always is. Results must be public outcomes, not organizational outcomes: what changes for citizens, not what your entity achieves. This single substitution is the difference between an application that reads as extractive and one that reads as aligned.

**Internal memo.** The hardest P to write honestly, because the consensus being challenged is your own team's, and often your own from eighteen months ago. Name it as yours. "We built the last two years on an assumption that has stopped being true" buys more credibility than any amount of framing.

**Landing page.** The only artifact where R may appear before P, as a logo strip or a metric bar. That is a credential placement to buy attention, not a reordering. The full Results node still lands after Execution.

## 4. Where the turn sits, by artifact

The turn is the sentence that pivots from broken to missed. It is the highest-value line in the piece and its placement is not negotiable.

- **Deck:** the third slide, as the only line on it.
- **Essay:** end of the second or third paragraph, after the stakes and before the argument opens.
- **Keynote:** minute 3 to 4, after the tension is felt physically and before attention decays.
- **Email:** sentence two.
- **Memo:** the end of the first paragraph. Executives read one paragraph.
- **Landing page:** the subhead.
- **Thread:** post 2 or 3, never post 1, since post 1 buys the click.

If a draft has no identifiable turn sentence, it has no Insight node, whatever the outline claims.
