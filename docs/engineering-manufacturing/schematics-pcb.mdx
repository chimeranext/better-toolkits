# Schematics & PCB Design

The Schematics & PCB Design toolkit covers the electrical design of hardware and
IoT products: capturing schematics, laying out printed circuit boards, checking
designs against rules, and producing the files a fabricator needs. Use it when
you design, review, or release PCBs.

This is a **playbook**: it explains *when and why* to use each skill. For the
executable steps, open the skill itself (the runbook).

## When to use this toolkit

Use Schematics & PCB Design when the work is electrical board design:

- You structure a KiCad project.
- You capture or review a schematic.
- You lay out a printed circuit board.
- You run electrical rule checks (ERC) and design rule checks (DRC).
- You export Gerbers, drill files, BOM, and pick-and-place data.
- You compare two netlists to confirm a change.
- You need researched, structured content to train or document a hardware topic.

## Core skills — when and why

| Skill | When to use it | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **schematics-pcb-design** | you capture a schematic or lay out a PCB | it gives KiCad structure, schematic rules, PCB layout guidelines, ERC/DRC checklists, and manufacturing outputs |
| **deep-research** | you need researched, structured content for training or documentation | it combines web and crawled sources into organized Markdown chapters |

## When NOT to use this toolkit

- For mechanical parts, machining, or tolerances — use the Manufacturing & Robotics toolkit.
- For CAD, 3D modeling, or digital twins — use the 3D Graphics & CAD toolkit.
- For firmware or software development — use the software engineering toolkit.
- For component procurement, customs, or importing — use the Logistics toolkit.
